1. Five projects, one operating system

Kevin Unk Ai · 81 minutes · published July 13, 2026 · source. Lesson written from the public transcript and chapters. Not a re-upload of the video.

This is the map video. He is not teaching one trick. He is showing five pieces he wants to run as one desk: a rented graphics-card computer, a local model, Hermes, Claude Code, and a dashboard.

What you are looking at

A VPS is a computer you rent by the hour. He uses SimplePod. On screen he picks a large card, an RTX Pro 6000 with 96 GB of video memory, listed around $1.39 an hour in the transcript summary, Ubuntu 24.04, and an SSH key so he can log in from his laptop. Storage stays when the machine is paused. The point of the rent is to avoid buying a several-thousand-dollar rig before the loop works.

VRAM is the card's memory. It decides which model file can sit on the card. He says a roughly 30 billion parameter model, shrunk well, can be more useful than a bigger file that does not fit. Quantization is the shrink. He tells people Q6 or cleaner for tool calling, and Q4 only for chat. That matches the rule for your reports: do not use the fuzzier copy when the output has to stay on the notes.

The two programs

Claude Code is the builder in the terminal. The transcript says it still needs an Anthropic login, about $20 a month. He uses it to harden the rented server. Hermes, from Nous Research, is the night program. He names a Hermes around 30B, and later a Hermes 4.3 around 36B in FP8, started with systemd so it stays up. Ollama is the program that holds the model files. Codex is mentioned as a second coding agent. OpenCode is named as a later add, a command-line agent.

What the dashboard is

From about the 33-minute mark he shows a phone and web screen: voice, a Kanban board, memory, and file navigation. Memory is described as more than one pile: notes, a graph, relationships, and SQLite. He also shows a separate mentor app built from a question list and a fine-tune dataset. That app is his product pitch. It is not required for an incident draft.

What he says is not finished

Around 46 minutes he scores the stack about 8 out of 10 as a machine and about 6.5 to 7 as something he could sell. Missing pieces he names: mobile polish, picking the right agent, a panel that shows what happened, and written procedures. At the end he uses a six-question frame for a task: who, where, what, why, how, when.

What to copy

Rent or use one machine. One model file. One builder. A dashboard that only reads files. You sign the output. Do not copy the fine-tune of his personal dataset, and do not treat the hourly price on screen as your bill until you check SimplePod yourself.

2. Eight jobs on Hermes Desktop

Kevin Unk Ai · about 46 minutes · source. Written from the public transcript.

Hermes Agent is the runtime. Hermes Desktop is the window. He points the window at Minimax M3 through OpenRouter, then asks it to propose eight jobs from his contacts and memory.

The eight jobs

  1. Revenue intelligence. What is making money, written to a file.
  2. Content repurposing. One source becomes a pack.
  3. Video production. The pack becomes a clip package.
  4. Offer builder. The pack becomes an offer draft.
  5. Social routing. The pack is assigned to a platform.
  6. Lead follow-up. A person who replied gets a next step.
  7. System reliability. Did the run fail.
  8. Memory librarian. What should be kept.

He splits them into a money loop and a support loop, and he says not to add a ninth until these eight write files you can open. He calls extra agents profile soup.

The handoff

The order he asks for is revenue, then content pack, then offer draft, then video package, then social router, then lead follow-up. Each stage writes markdown or JSON. The next stage may only read that file. He runs it by hand, then talks about a cron schedule. Hyperframes is named as the tool that turns HTML into a short video. Telegram is named as a message door. MCP is named as the plug shape for extra tools. Apify is named as a later scan tool, not as something he finishes on screen.

What to copy

Eight text files, or fewer. One writes. The next reads. You approve before a timer. For a security desk the same shape is notes in, draft out, you sign. Do not start with eight.

3. The drop folder

Flux | AI Expert · 14 minutes · July 20, 2026 · source. Written from the public chapters and transcript.

One video goes in a folder. A watcher sees it. Claude Code resizes it, writes a caption, and a timer posts it. The 500-posts line is his claim. The transcript does not show a log of 500 posts.

Folders he names

  • inbox, where you drop the file
  • posting, or in process, while it runs
  • published, after a post succeeds
  • failed, when it does not
  • a raw folder for long videos that get cut into shorts

Setup he actually walks

Instagram goes through a Meta developer app, a business account, a tester invite, and a token with publish permissions. YouTube goes through Google Cloud, the YouTube Data API, and a desktop OAuth client. He does not walk TikTok, X, or LinkedIn token setup even though the title lists them. The timer example is a cron job at 3 p.m. Assets sit on store.fluxgrowthagency.com, not a public GitHub.

What to copy

Four folders and a human look at the first output. Do not put tokens in a screenshot. Do not post a client's clip on a timer until you have watched one full run.

He describes scraping local businesses, building a mock site, and emailing it. The thousand-task line, the 99 percent token cut, and the $12,000 dental estimate are said, not shown. Copy the split of cheap model versus careful model. Do not copy the cold-email volume.
